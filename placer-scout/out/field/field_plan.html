Placer Scout field plan

Driving, drone and walking plan for the 25 wash targets and the 50 ant-mound clip spots. Each stop has a launch point on a public road, a drone shot list with exact start and stop points, what to check on foot, and warnings for private land, claims and old shafts.

Where to start

  1. Trip A (Nelson) first. It is the closest, the walks are short, and it answers the two biggest open questions in one morning: are the ant "clearings" real, and is the #1 target a real gold trap?
  2. Trip B (Searchlight) next. Ten targets, mostly right off the road. Several sit tight against private land and old shafts, so watch the red and amber warnings.
  3. Trip C (Goodsprings) last. Longer, rougher walks. Skip #16 and #18 unless you're set up for a real hike.

Bring the files from the chat: field_plan.kml (launch points, targets and wash flight lines for Google Earth or a GPS app), clip_spots.gpx and targets.gpx.

Data that raises confidence

Record these at every stop. They let me check the model against what's really on the ground and re-weight it.

Waypoint and time
Where you stood, and when.
Photo and video file names
Matched to the stop code (A1, B3…).
Trap check
Is the junction, widening or slope break real? Yes, no or partly.
Bedrock
Exposed in the channel? Depth if you probed.
Gravel
Mostly sand, pea gravel, cobbles or boulders.
Pans
Gold colors in each of 3 pans, plus black sand: none, some or lots.
Flood signs
Debris line height, fresh scour, uprooted brush.
Ant nests
Clip number or waypoint, disc size, ants active or not.
Access
Road condition, gates, signs, claim posts you saw.

Zero colors is a useful result too: it tells me which signals to trust less.

Rules and safety

  • Drone: you need the FAA TRUST certificate. Stay under 400 ft and keep the drone in sight. Check B4UFLY before each trip.
  • No-drone zones: no flights in Lake Mead NRA (Park Service). Don't launch or land inside wilderness. Keep well clear of bighorn sheep and desert tortoises.
  • Panning: hand panning on open BLM land only. Never on an active claim or private land; claim posts and signs override this plan.
  • Old mines: never enter old shafts or tunnels. Stay off dump edges, and walk where you can see the ground.
  • Mill waste: old mill tailings can hold mercury and arsenic. Don't dig in them or breathe their dust, and wash your hands and gear.
  • Weather: no wash work if storms are possible anywhere upstream. Check the NWS Las Vegas forecast that morning.
  • Heat: go early. Bring plenty of water, and tell someone your route; many spots have no cell signal.

Trip A · Nelson / Eldorado

Start here. Closest to Las Vegas, all stops are short walks, and it settles the ant-mound question while checking the #1 target.

From Henderson, take US-95 south toward Searchlight, then turn east on NV-165 (Nelson Road) toward Nelson. The stops sit along NV-165 and the dirt roads north and south of it, before the road drops into Eldorado Canyon. Lower Eldorado Canyon and the river are Lake Mead NRA: no drones there.

  • coarse gravel on the inside of the bend just below the junction;

Take 3 test pans: just below the junction, at the head of the gravel bar, and from a bedrock crack if you find one. Count the gold colors in each pan and note the black sand.

A3

Ant mound clip area (50 spots)

Launchunnamed road (local road) · 35.69744, -114.86704
Walk685 m (2,247 ft) walk, 75 m climb · moderate
Target35.69149, -114.86502
Navigate to launchTarget on map

Drone: one photo per spot

  1. Load clip_spots.gpx (50 waypoints) into your phone or flight app.
  2. At each spot, hover about 35 m up and take one straight-down photo. Name or note it by clip number.
  3. For any spot that shows a round bare patch, drop to 15 m and take two more straight-down photos.

The spots spread over about 1.2 × 1.5 km, so work them in a loop and expect 2–3 batteries.

On foot

Walk to any spot that looks like a clearing. A harvester-ant nest has a bare disc, a central hole and ant traffic. Look, don't dig: these ants sting. Run a magnet or detector over the cleared surface and the tailings ring around the hole.
