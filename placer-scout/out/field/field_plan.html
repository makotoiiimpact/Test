Placer Scout field plan

Driving, drone and walking plan for the top 8 wash targets: the strong tier, each below a recorded gold mine, mill or mine dump. Each stop has a launch point on a public road, a drone shot list with exact start and stop points, what to check on foot, and warnings for private land, claims and old shafts.

Where to start

Best bets after the old-map check: #2 (Eldorado Canyon/Nelson), #3 (Searchlight) and #6 (Goodsprings). Each sits in a real wash just below a named gold mine. The 1950s maps show #4, #5 and #7 sit on quarries under a power line, an old landing strip by the highway and an open slope, so look at those from the road or skip them.

  1. Trip C · Goodsprings (4 targets, best is #1): longer, rougher walks; go early with plenty of water.
  2. Trip A · Nelson / Eldorado (2 targets, best is #2): the #1 target, closest to Las Vegas.
  3. Trip B · Searchlight (1 target, best is #3): short walks, but tight against private land and old shafts; read the warnings.
  4. Trip D · Other (1 target, best is #4): one target at the south edge of the valley.

The ant-mound check is off the list: in the clip review none of the 50 spots looked like a real clearing, so the photo scan stopped there. Bring the files from the chat: field_plan.kml (launch points, targets and wash flight lines for Google Earth or a GPS app) and targets.gpx.

After this week's rain

  • Wait 2–3 dry days after heavy rain before going into any wash. Beds stay soft, banks undercut and collapse, and dirt roads wash out or turn to mud.
  • Check the forecast the morning you go. Look at the NWS Las Vegas forecast and any flash flood watch. Rain miles upstream can send a flood down a dry wash under clear sky.
  • Never drive into water across a road or wash dip. Turn around.
  • Why it's worth the wait: fresh floods wash sand off the gravel bars and can leave bedrock bare. Look at the upstream ends of bars, the inside of bends below junctions, and cracks in newly exposed bedrock.
  • Fly after it dries: the drone video right after a flood season is the best before-and-after record you'll get. Note the date of the storm you're following.

Rules and safety

  • Drone: you need the FAA TRUST certificate. Stay under 400 ft and keep the drone in sight. Check B4UFLY before each trip.
  • No-drone zones: no flights in Lake Mead NRA (Park Service). Don't launch or land inside wilderness. Keep well clear of bighorn sheep and desert tortoises.
  • Panning: hand panning on open BLM land only. Never on an active claim or private land; claim posts and signs override this plan.
  • Old mines: never enter old shafts or tunnels. Stay off dump edges, and walk where you can see the ground.
  • Mill waste: old mill tailings can hold mercury and arsenic. Don't dig in them or breathe their dust, and wash your hands and gear.
  • Weather: no wash work if storms are possible anywhere upstream. Check the NWS Las Vegas forecast that morning.
  • Heat: go early. Bring plenty of water, and tell someone your route; many spots have no cell signal.

Trip A · Nelson / Eldorado

Closest to Las Vegas, and it holds the #1 target.

From Henderson, take US-95 south toward Searchlight, then turn east on NV-165 (Nelson Road) toward Nelson. The stops sit along NV-165 and the dirt roads north and south of it, before the road drops into Eldorado Canyon. Lower Eldorado Canyon and the river are Lake Mead NRA: no drones there. Long hikes: Target #2 (0.6 km, 82 m climb).

Drone shot list

  1. Overview. Climb to about 60 m over the target. Take one straight-down photo and one angled shot looking upstream.
  2. Wash video. Start upstream at 35.71208, -114.82921 and fly down the wash to 35.71042, -114.82625 (about 350 m). Stay about 25 m over the wash, camera 45–60° down, 2–3 m/s. Then fly the same line back at 40 m, camera straight down, a photo every 2 seconds.
  3. Close-ups. Hover 12–15 m over the target (widening) and take straight-down photos of the junction or bar, plus any rock ribs crossing the channel and dark black-sand streaks.

The target is about level with the launch point.

On foot

Walk to the target only after the flight, and stay on BLM ground. Look for:

  • bedrock showing in the channel floor;
  • coarse gravel on the inside of the bend just below the junction;
  • a debris line from the last flood.

Take 3 test pans: just below the junction, at the head of the gravel bar, and from a bedrock crack if you find one. Count the gold colors in each pan and note the black sand.

1950s USGS map check: weak

Beside the old Nelson landing strip and the highway at the edge of town, on a small side channel; likely road and airstrip drainage.

Trip B · Searchlight

Mostly short walks. Several sit right against private land and old shafts, so read each card's warnings.

Take US-95 south from Henderson to Searchlight. The targets are on the edge of town and along Cottonwood Cove Road east of US-95. Avi Kwa Ame National Monument surrounds this area: stay on the targets listed, which are outside it.

Trip C · Goodsprings

Longer walks and rougher ground. Do it after A and B, early in the day, with plenty of water.

Take I-15 south to Jean, then NV-161 west to Goodsprings. Most targets are on dirt roads in the hills west and north of town. Some need a high-clearance vehicle.
